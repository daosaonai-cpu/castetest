Study Guide: Indian Mathematics

1. Numbers, Fractions and Geometry in the Vedas

The Vedas, the oldest layer of Sanskrit literature, contain early mathematical concepts. The Sulba Sutras (appendices to the Vedas) are primarily known for geometry, detailing the construction of complex fire altars using precise geometric principles. They demonstrate knowledge of the Pythagorean theorem, calculating square roots, and using specific fractions like ardha (1/2), pada (1/4), and tri-bhaga (1/3).

2. Decimal Nomenclature in the Vedas

Vedic Indians developed a systematic decimal nomenclature, assigning unique names to powers of 10 up to extremely large numbers. The sequence includes: Eka (10^0), Dasha (10^1), Shata (10^2), Sahasra (10^3), Ayuta (10^4), Niyuta (10^5), Prayuta (10^6), Arbuda (10^7), and extends to Parardha (10^12). This linguistic foundation paved the way for the mathematical place-value system.

3. Zero and Infinity

The concept of zero, called Shunya (meaning void or empty), originated in India. Initially used as a placeholder (a dot or bindu), it was formalized as a mathematical number by Brahmagupta in 628 CE. The concept of infinity (Ananta or Purnam) is deeply philosophical and mathematical, explicitly mentioned in the Isha Upanishad, which states that extracting infinity from infinity leaves infinity.

4. Development of the Decimal Place Value System

India is the birthplace of the modern decimal place-value system (Hindu-Arabic numeral system). Using just ten symbols (0-9) and positional value, this system dramatically simplified all arithmetical operations compared to older systems (like Roman numerals). This allowed for efficient addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

5. Linguistic Representation of Numbers

To memorize vast numerical tables and constants in verse, ancient scholars invented systems to map numbers to words or letters:

  • Bhuta-Sankhya System: Uses familiar objects to represent numbers (e.g., 'Eyes' or 'Netra' = 2, 'Vedas' = 4, 'Moon' = 1).
  • Katapayadi System: Maps Sanskrit consonants to digits (1-9 and 0), allowing numbers to be encoded into meaningful poetry.

6. Important Texts of Indian Mathematics

Key historical texts and their authors include:

  • Aryabhatiya (by Aryabhata): Covered arithmetic, algebra, and plane trigonometry.
  • Brahmasphutasiddhanta (by Brahmagupta): First text to give rules for zero and negative numbers.
  • Lilavati & Bijaganita (by Bhaskaracharya / Bhaskara II): Masterpieces on arithmetic and algebra, respectively.

7. Algebra, Trigonometry, and Calculus

  • Algebra (Bijaganita/Avyaktaganita): Focused on the 'mathematics of the unmanifest' (unknown variables). Included advanced solutions to quadratic and indeterminate equations (like the Chakravala method).
  • Trigonometry: Originated from astronomical needs. Aryabhata introduced Jya (Sine) and Koti-jya (Cosine).
  • Calculus: The Kerala School (founded by Madhava of Sangamagrama in the 14th century) discovered infinite series for Pi, Sine, and Cosine centuries before Newton and Leibniz.
